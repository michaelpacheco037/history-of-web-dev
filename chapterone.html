01 Chapter 1 / 1989–1999

The early years

The first website, early browsers, and the beginnings of a new medium.

1989–1991

A new way to share information

Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web at CERN in 1989. By the end of 1990, he had built its first browser and server. In 1991, the project began reaching people beyond CERN.

The first website explained the web itself: how to find documents, follow links, and set up a server. A collection of connected pages was a small idea with enormous possibilities.

1992–1994

More people find their way online

Early browsers helped people explore this growing collection of documents. In 1993, NCSA released Mosaic, a graphical browser that helped introduce the web to a wider audience.

That same year, CERN released its web software into the public domain. Developers could use and improve it freely, helping an open web grow beyond its original scientific community.

1995–1999

The web becomes a place to do things

As businesses moved online, websites became places to shop, search, and discover services. Personal homepages also gave everyday users a space to experiment with text, images, and links.

JavaScript and CSS expanded what a page could do and how it could look. The next chapter follows those two important changes.

Internet ≠ World Wide Web

The internet is the network connecting computers. The web is one service that uses that network to connect pages and other resources through links.
